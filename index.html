National trends

In 2024, road travel per resident was close to its 2000 level. Highway fuel use per 1,000 vehicle miles was lower. Over the full period, the decline in fuel use per resident appears to be driven more by lower fuel use per mile than by a long-term decline in road travel.

Definitions and sources

How to read the measures

Road travel per resident (miles per resident)

FHWA vehicle miles traveled on public roads divided by resident population. It includes cars, trucks, buses, motorcycles, freight and visitor traffic. It is a measure of road travel relative to population, not the number of miles driven by the average resident.

Highway fuel use (gallons per 1,000 VMT)

FHWA highway gasoline plus highway special fuel divided by FHWA road VMT. Lower values mean fewer reported gallons are used for the same amount of road travel. The measure can reflect vehicle efficiency, vehicle mix, freight activity and other factors. It is not the same as passenger-vehicle MPG.

Gasoline consistency ratio (%)

FHWA highway gasoline divided by EIA transportation-sector motor gasoline. EIA's transportation gasoline measure is broader than highway-only gasoline, so the ratio is a reconciliation check rather than a literal “share of transportation on highways.”

Why FHWA and EIA are both used

FHWA provides the road-travel and highway-fuel data used in the main analysis. EIA provides broader transportation energy and emissions data. At this stage, EIA is used here for the gasoline consistency comparison. Additional emissions measures will be added as the analysis develops.

Project data

FHWA MF-21 highway motor-fuel use; FHWA VM-2 vehicle miles traveled; EIA SEDS motor gasoline; EIA SEDS population. Historical backup fills from FHWA MF-225 and MF-226 are retained where the main MF-21 series was missing.
